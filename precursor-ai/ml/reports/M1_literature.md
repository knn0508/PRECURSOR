## Findings

- **F1 No NISAR-landslide ML study found.** Searches found only L-band deformation work in general (coherence in vegetation). NISAR-based landslide prediction has no published precedent here: NOT_VERIFIED that none exists.
- **F2 Labels are the bottleneck**, not models: Stanley 2021 calls even the best inventories far from complete; evaluation was a temporal hold-out.
- **F3 Validation design matters** (papers 7-9, snippets): spatial blocking and harder background sampling lower scores; the track's rules already require both. Sensitivity of results to background sampling goes to M8.
- **F4 Tabular boosting baseline is plausible** but the evidence in this file is snippet-level (6) plus one READ data-driven XGBoost precedent (1). Let M5/M7 decide.
- **F5 Prompts.md reading list partly unverified**: items 6, 7, 9, 10 still need a human or a different fetch route (institution proxy, PDF text extraction) to be read in full.

Implications used in M5: (a) random splits and random CV inflate scores and are not used (papers 18, 19, 20 use random splits; 20 shows the effect in simulation); (b) for tabular data of this size tree ensembles and regularised linear models are the defensible families (16), deep models need far more data and are fragile to uninformative features; (c) the GLC literature itself filters events by location accuracy (15 uses 10 km), consistent with the M4 rule; (d) space-time models need multi-temporal inventories with dated, complete maps (17), which this inventory is not.
