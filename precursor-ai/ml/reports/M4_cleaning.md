## 2. Ocean / no-data cells: outcome-blind exclusion
The exclusion function receives **feature columns only** (a test proves that adding or shuffling label columns cannot change it, and the real-data test recomputes it from the label-free table). Criteria, fixed before looking at labels: (a) water + ocean share of the cell above **0.5**, where ocean = 1 - WorldCover valid share and water = valid share x water class; (b) elevation coverage below 0.5; (c) soil cover below 0.1 **and** land-cover valid share below 0.1. Result: **1,685 of 10,276 cells excluded on the 5 km grid** (CA_S 1,285, CA_N 294, OR_WA 106; 1,137 by the water/ocean rule alone, 548 by it together with no soil/no land cover) and **432 of 2,571 on the 10 km grid**. Criterion (b) excludes nothing, because NASADEM records the sea as elevation 0 instead of no data; the water/ocean fraction is what detects sea.
**Events in excluded cells are reported, not dropped:** among the 647 clean strict events, **62 (5 km) / 96 (10 km)** fall in excluded cells (5 km: 53 in water/ocean cells, 9 in cells outside the window mask). They stay in `events_clean.parquet` with `in_excluded_cell = True`; whether to use them is for M5/M6.
**With/without sensitivity** (`exclusion_sensitivity.json`; descriptive only): water threshold 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.7 -> 5 km cells excluded 1,818 / 1,685 / 1,592, clean strict events in excluded cells 103 / 62 / 28, point-cell rate (cells with an event point) all cells 4.05% vs kept cells 4.21% / 4.45% / 4.61%; 10 km: cells excluded 477 / 432 / 383, events in excluded cells 142 / 96 / 25, point-cell rate 11.4% vs 12.3% / 12.8% / 13.3%. The largest Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance between the feature distributions of all cells and kept cells (slope, elevation, rainfall, NDVI, clay, tree cover) is 0.14 to 0.15 at every threshold on both grids: removing sea cells changes the distributions moderately, mainly through the coastal windows. Exclusion therefore matters for CA_S in particular.

## 3. Feature QC
- **Physical/definitional ranges** (elevation -100..6,000 m, slope 0-90, shares 0-1, texture 0-100, NDVI -1..1, rainfall and distances >= 0, seasonality index <= 1.84): **0 violations**, 0 fill-like values (<= -9,000) in any of the 72 factors.
- **Composition checks:** land-cover shares and lithology shares each sum to 1 within 1.2e-7; clay + sand + silt per cell is 99.93 to 100.07 (100% within 97-103), which also supports the SoilGrids scaling.
- **Outliers** are flagged by robust z-score (|x - median| / (1.4826 MAD) > 6), **never deleted**: counts are in `feature_qc.json`. They are large and uninformative for zero-inflated shares (e.g. cropland 4,459, unconsolidated 4,368 cells) because the MAD is near zero; no value was changed or removed.
- **NDVI georeferencing QC (all 240 granules, no download):** for every file the tile in the file name equals the tile numbers in the file's own tags, the acquisition month equals the manifest, and the West/North/South bounding coordinates equal the sinusoidal tile arithmetic exactly (difference 0.0000 degrees). The East bounding coordinate differs by a constant 0.0096 (h07v05, h08v05) to 0.0109 (h08v04, h09v04) degrees (about 1 km), a systematic edge convention rather than a displaced tile (a wrong tile would be off by about 10 degrees). Tolerance used: 0.02 degrees (documented; a test shows the offset appears when the tolerance is tightened). **0 of 240 disagree, so no granule is excluded from the NDVI statistics.**
- **SoilGrids units:** the applied conversions (clay, sand, silt: g/kg divided by 10 = %; coarse fragments: vol permille divided by 10 = vol%) match the official ISRIC table on https://docs.isric.org/globaldata/soilgrids/SoilGrids_faqs_01.html ("SoilGrids layers", last updated 2026-09-02): clay/sand/silt mapped unit g/kg, factor 10, conventional g/100g (%); cfvo mapped cm3/dm3 (vol permille), factor 10, conventional cm3/100cm3 (vol%); the page states "dividing the predictions values by the values in the Conversion factor column" gives the conventional units. **VERIFIED.** (The page does not describe the tile data type; Int16 and nodata -32,768 come from the tile VRT read in M2b.) Evidence file: `soilgrids_units_check.json`.

## 5. Background sampling (pseudo-absences)
Rule as stated by the user: background cells lie in the same window, are not excluded, and their **whole cell** is farther than max(2 x accuracy radius, 1 cell) from **every** event (every located GLC row, whatever its trigger or accuracy; unknown accuracy buffered as 5 km, an assumption). Candidates are split into 5 terrain strata by slope quantiles, ranked by a seeded permutation, and the **primary draw takes ratio 5 per clean strict event** (quota per stratum proportional to its candidates; all candidates stay ranked so any other ratio can be drawn).

**Result: the rule as stated leaves very few candidates.** 61 events in the windows have accuracy radii of 25 or 50 km (buffers of 50 and 100 km), which cover most of a 3 degree window. **5 km grid: 1,319 candidates (CA_N 1,049, CA_S 269, OR_WA 1); 1,130 selected** (target 5 x 647 = 3,235; shortfalls CA_S 531, OR_WA 1,574); strata 226 / 226 / 225 / 226 / 227. **10 km grid: 261 candidates (CA_N 216, CA_S 45, OR_WA 0)**. So Oregon/Washington, the window with the most events (315), has effectively no background under this rule.
**Recorded variant** (file `background_candidates_variant_buffer_le10km.parquet`): only events with accuracy up to 10 km shield their surroundings (events less precise than that are not used for buffers). **5 km: 4,772 candidates (CA_N 2,209, CA_S 1,386, OR_WA 1,177), 2,837 selected (OR_WA short by 398); 10 km: 901 candidates (CA_N 444, CA_S 261, OR_WA 196)**, ratio 5 still unreachable on the 10 km grid. The choice between the literal rule and the variant is a decision for you (section 10); the variant weakens the guarantee that background cells are far from every possible event location.
**Caveat (both):** background cells are places with no catalog record, **not** proven stable ground; the GLC is incomplete and biased to reported, accessible places. The positive-cell rule remains open for M6.

## 7b. Code review
A reviewer subagent audited `m4.py`, `m4_clean.py` and the tests. **Two bugs were found and fixed**: (1) near-duplicate detection used single linkage, so a chain A-B-C (800 m steps) merged events 1,600 m apart; it is now greedy against kept events only; (2) background buffers skipped events less precise than 10 km, contrary to the rule "outside 2x the accuracy radius of every event"; the rule is now literal and the capped version is kept as a recorded variant. **Risks fixed**: wall-clock "today" in the date rule (now `as_of_date` in the config); merges without cardinality checks; duplicate keeper chosen among rows already rejected for other reasons; in-period bounds hard-coded (now read from the M3 config); no event-set equality check between the M3 rebuild and the cleaned table; the prior-rain check covered only the 1-month column; the target-derived scan covered only prefixed columns; the real-data audit accepted any WARN; an empty NDVI QC could pass silently; the temporal split cut inside a day. **Open question answered**: events whose cell lies outside the window mask stay in the clean strict set but are flagged `in_excluded_cell` (they have no features), counted in all exclusion reports. All counts in this report are from the re-run after the fixes.
## 8. Caveats carried forward
NDVI 2013-2017 covers 345 of the 667 strict events (335 for 2014-2017 only); land cover is 2021 and soil is a static model product; the 667 events fall on 321 dates with 185 in 2017 (temporal structure to be examined in M5 before any temporal hold-out); the GLC is news-derived and incomplete; the meaning of the "5km" accuracy class is NOT_VERIFIED; bulk density, SMAP, daily rainfall, SAR and roads are not part of the dataset.

## 9. What was not verified
Whether near-duplicates (same day, within 1 km) are truly the same report rather than two separate slides (a conservative convention; 20 strict events were affected); that "unknown" triggers (371 events) are mostly rain-triggered, which would make the rain-type filter drop real events; the 0.5 water threshold (sensitivity shown, not optimised); whether events less precise than 10 km should shield their surroundings; the soil data-stream date; that the East-bound offset in the NDVI tags is only a convention (checked by consistency across 240 files, not by VIIRS documentation).
