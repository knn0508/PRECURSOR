## 2. Processing choices and their justification
1. **Analysis grid: 5 km cells** (user condition: cell size consistent with the ~5 km GLC accuracy; no 30 m modelling with these points). 5 km equals the largest "strict" accuracy class. Reference grid from Phase 4 `build_reference_grid` (UTM zone from the window centroid, origin snapped to multiples of 5 km, so identical inputs give identical grids). Cells whose centre is outside the window are NaN (`inside_aoi` band).
2. **Label ambiguity is large even at 5 km, and this is the main M3 finding.** An event with 5 km accuracy can lie in any cell touched by a 5 km circle: 7-9 cells at 5 km. Share of strict events whose accuracy circle touches more than one cell: 61% (OR_WA, CA_N) to 68% (CA_S) at 5 km; 76-82% at 1 km; 43-56% at 10 km; 29-37% at 20 km (`grid_sensitivity.json`). Distinct point cells shrink as cells grow (OR_WA 272 -> 189 -> 128 -> 75 for 1, 5, 10, 20 km). M3 therefore does **not** decide the positive-cell definition: it records, per event, the accuracy radius, the cell holding the point, the number of cells the circle touches, and builds two label layers per set (strict all years, strict in period): `label_point_count` (events whose point lies in the cell) and `label_weight` (each event spreads weight 1 over the cells its circle touches; unknown accuracy gets no cells). The choice (point cell, weighted, or coarser cell) is for M6 and the user, with sensitivity runs in M8.
3. **Terrain** from the 30 m NASADEM mosaic (27 tiles, 9 per window; no tile missing) using the Phase 4 Horn gradients, aspect and slope functions, imported read-only and run in 1-degree latitude strips with a one-row halo (tested identical to one pass) because only about 3 GB of RAM was free. Fine-scale values are aggregated to the 5 km cell by area averaging (GDAL warp average, not bilinear, to avoid aliasing): mean/min/max/std of elevation, relief (max - min), mean, 3rd quartile and max of the 30 m slope, share of 30 m cells steeper than 15 and 30 degrees, circular aspect components (mean sin, mean cos, resultant length), DEM cover. Note: "slope_mean" is the mean of 30 m slopes, not the slope at 5 km scale. Curvature, TWI and flow accumulation were **not** computed: not justified at a 5 km cell (the prompt says "only if justified").
4. **Land cover**: WorldCover 2021 v200 10 m classes counted per ~100 m block (10 x 10 pixels, exact; 0 = no data), averaged onto the 5 km cell, expressed as the share of valid pixels (`lc_<class>`) plus `lc_valid_frac` (ocean is no data). Categorical data are never interpolated; shares are area-based. Single-year snapshot (2021) against events from 1997-2017: **temporal mismatch**, recorded as a limitation.
5. **Lithology**: one documented class system (`LITH_CLASSES`, 9 classes) built on the field **GENERALIZE**, which the three state files share. The state unit codes (`ORIG_LABEL`, `SGMC_LABEL`) differ by state and are NOT mapped. 24 distinct GENERALIZE values occur across the three states; every one maps (`lithology_mapping.json`). **Unmapped classes: none** in any window (checked on all polygons read). Mapping: Igneous volcanic -> IGN_VOLCANIC; Igneous intrusive and undifferentiated igneous -> IGN_INTRUSIVE; all "Metamorphic, ..." except serpentinite -> METAMORPHIC; Metamorphic serpentinite -> SERPENTINITE (kept apart because of its slope-instability relevance in California); Sedimentary clastic and undifferentiated sedimentary -> SED_CLASTIC; Sedimentary carbonate/chemical -> SED_CARBONATE_CHEMICAL; Unconsolidated -> UNCONSOLIDATED; the mixed "X and Y, undifferentiated" classes and Melange -> MIXED_UNDIFFERENTIATED; Water and Ice -> WATER_ICE. Polygons are rasterised at ~100 m (no resampling of class ids), then area-averaged to shares of the covered area (`lith_cover` = covered share; no data where no state map exists, e.g. Mexico and open ocean in CA_S). Merging the finer metamorphic classes loses detail (documented). The relation of these files to SGMC v1.1 is NOT_VERIFIED; the shapefiles carry `SGMC_LABEL` and links to the USGS SGMC unit pages, which is suggestive, not proof.
6. **Rainfall (static climatology only)**: IMERG V07 Final monthly rate (mm/hr) read through GDAL's HDF5 driver (array order [lon, lat], registration at cell centres, origin south-west; fill -9999.9 -> NaN), converted to mm/month with the UTC calendar-month length. Climatology over the full calendar years 2002-2017 (16 years): mean annual total, December-February and June-August means, wettest climatological month, interannual coefficient of variation, Walsh-Lawler seasonality index. Bilinear to the grid; the native 0.1 deg field is coarser than the cell, so the warning "OVERSAMPLING: no detail is added" applies to all rainfall bands. Orientation check passed by test: OR_WA (mean 1,702 mm/yr) > CA_N (737) > CA_S (266), and winter-dominated in California (JJA a few mm).
7. **Time indexing**: everything is UTC; monthly series are keyed by UTC month start. For each event the monthly rainfall of the **1 and 3 calendar months strictly before the event month** is attached (`rain_prev_1m_mm`, `rain_prev_3m_mm`, nearest 0.1 deg cell). The event month itself is deliberately NOT provided (it contains post-event days; leakage). 18 strict events (all dated before 2001-09, i.e. before the IMERG record starts) have no prior-month rainfall (NaN, not 0).
8. **Labels**: all GLC rows inside each window are kept with flags (`usable_base`: dated, rain-type trigger, landslide-type category; `strict_all_years` = usable and accuracy in {exact, 1km, 5km}; `strict_in_period` = also inside 2014-10-03..2025-09-30), radius in metres (0 / 1,000 / 5,000 / 10,000 / ...; NaN for "unknown"), UTM coordinates, grid row/col. No event was dropped or repaired; cleaning belongs to M4. The 15,274 COOLR new events fall in none of the three windows (count 0 each), so labels are GLC only.

## 3. Real results worth knowing (not model results)
- **Event clustering is heavy**: 667 strict events fall on 321 distinct dates; the largest single day holds 25; years with the most events: 2017 (185), 2010 (112), 2011 (100), 2014 (61), 2015 (51). Few winters dominate. M5 must examine this before any temporal hold-out is decided (user condition).
- Strict accuracy mix: 1 km 300, 5 km 219, exact 148. Point cells with at least one strict event (inside cells): 120 (CA_N), 113 (CA_S), 186 (OR_WA) of 10,276 cells (about 4%).
- **Ocean and no-data cells are included** (decision for M4): cells with WorldCover valid share below 0.5: CA_S 541, CA_N 47, OR_WA 0; lithology covered share below 0.5: CA_S 558, CA_N 1; cells where mapped lithology is mostly water: CA_S 1,000, CA_N 288, OR_WA 110 (these include coastal bays and the Pacific). Some elevations are negative (e.g. -71 m near the Salton Sea, a real depression).
- Aspect shares are NaN where all 30 m cells are flat (CA_S 68% valid, the rest flat or sea).

## 7. What was not verified
Scientific correctness of terrain values beyond plausibility and the synthetic ramp (no comparison with an independent slope product); WorldCover/lithology accuracy; the HDF5 array order was confirmed by the climatology pattern (wet Pacific North-West, dry southern California, dry summers), not by reading the dataset dimension names with h5py; NASADEM vertical datum (assumed, as in Phase 4); the GLC `location_accuracy` vocabulary meaning ("5km" assumed to be a radius, as the Phase 5 docs assume); relation of the state geology files to SGMC.

## 8. Risks
Positive-cell labels are ambiguous (section 2) and clustered in a few storms; the 5 km cell has about 10k cells inside, 4% with a strict point; land-cover year (2021) does not match event years; rainfall is 0.1 deg and only climatological; several literature-recommended factors are missing; coastal/ocean cells need an M4 rule.

## 10. M3b (round 2 factors), 2026-11-15
Acquisition: [M2b_acquisition](M2b_acquisition.md) (771 files, 3.57 GB). Code: [m3b.py](../src/precursor_ml/m3b.py), [m3b_process.py](../scripts/m3b_process.py), tests [test_m3b.py](../tests/test_m3b.py). Outputs per window next to the M3 files: `static_factors_r2.tif` (30 bands), `cells_r2.parquet`, `summary_r2.json`; combined `cells_all_full.parquet` (the 10,276 M3 cells with 30 more columns, one-to-one merge), `m3b_manifest.json` (hashes of all 771 inputs, rules, outputs). Built for the 5 km primary grid and the 10 km sensitivity grid (2,571 cells). The M3 products were not modified (hashes unchanged).

**Rules fixed before any label was looked at (outcome-blind):** rivers 'all' = every HydroRIVERS reach, 'major' = UPLAND_SKM >= 1,000 km2; faults 'all' = every trace of Qfaults_US_Database, 'young' = age in {historic, latest Quaternary, late Quaternary}; the California offshore fault layer is not used. Distances are computed in the window's UTM CRS: lines rasterised at 250 m (+50 km margin), Euclidean distance transform (error under about 180 m), then the **mean and minimum over each analysis cell** (bands `dist_<river|fault>_<all|major|young>_<mean|min>_m`, 8 bands). Soil: SoilGrids 250 m (Interrupted Goode Homolosine) tiles mosaicked per layer and **area-averaged** to the cell with GDAL average (nodata ignored), then mapped units divided by the documented factor (clay, sand, silt g/kg / 10 -> %, coarse fragments volume permille / 10 -> vol%); 12 layers (4 properties x 3 depths) plus the thickness-weighted 0-30 cm mean (weights 5/10/15 cm) per property and `soil_cover` (share of the cell with data): 17 bands. NDVI: VNP13A3 v002 monthly 1 km NDVI x 0.0001 kept where `pixel_reliability` is 0-2 and -1 <= NDVI <= 1; georeferenced by MODIS sinusoidal tile arithmetic (verified on one tile, see M2b report); statistics over the 60 months 2013-01..2017-12, pixels with fewer than 12 valid months are NaN (bands `ndvi_mean`, `ndvi_julaug`, `ndvi_janfeb`, `ndvi_amp` = seasonal amplitude of the monthly climatology, `ndvi_valid_month_share`), then 1 km pixels area-averaged to the cell: 5 bands.

**Caveats recorded:** (1) NDVI covers 2013-2017; **345 of the 667 strict events (335 for 2014-2017 only) fall in that period**, and the heavy 2010-2011 winters precede it; land cover is 2021 and soil is a static model product, so all vegetation/soil descriptors post-date or are unrelated in time to most events (report in M5). (2) The NDVI valid-month share is only 0.75 in OR_WA (cloud and snow); winter statistics there are weaker. (3) SoilGrids is modelled, 250 m, uncertain in mountains and forests. (4) HydroRIVERS is about 500 m accurate; mapped fault traces are incomplete and "distance to a fault" is an indirect proxy. (5) **Bulk density (`bdod`) was not acquired** (19 of 44 tiles return HTTP 404): MISSING_FACTOR pending the user's decision. (6) No road factor (not approved).
