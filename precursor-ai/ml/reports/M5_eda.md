## 0. The verdict in six lines
1. **The labels mostly describe where landslides get reported, not where slopes fail.** The strongest single-feature separations are reporting-bias proxies: built-up share (AUC 0.82; 0.97 in Oregon/Washington), absence of soil data (= urban or water, AUC 0.22), water share, coastal lithology. Physical drivers are weak and inconsistent: mean slope AUC 0.53 (95% block-bootstrap interval 0.43-0.62), and in Oregon/Washington the sign is **reversed** (slope AUC 0.18: events sit on flat urban ground).
2. **Effective sample size is small.** 460 training events, 270 training event-cells, but only **53 occupied 50 km blocks** (the effective number of independent spatial units) and 254 distinct dates. Events cluster strongly in space (Clark-Evans R 0.34-0.59; Moran's I of event cells 0.13-0.28) and in a few storms.
3. **Feasible and justified:** the static susceptibility task with regularised logistic regression and shallow, regularised tree ensembles on a small curated feature set, evaluated by spatial block CV, with explicit reporting-bias handling. **Not supported by this data:** any trigger / early-warning model (only monthly rainfall, no daily series, no lead time), deep models (CNN, LSTM, TCN, transformers), anything using InSAR (none acquired), and any claim of calibrated probabilities.
4. Expected skill is **modest and must be judged against a prior and a one- to three-feature baseline**; a model that mostly re-learns built-up share is not a susceptibility model.
5. Compute is **CPU only**, a few CPU-hours (estimate, not measured).
6. Two data issues need your decision (section 11): how to treat the reporting-bias proxies, and whether to pay for a better bias covariate (population density or roads).

## 1. The sealed provisional hold-out (created before any exploration)

## 5. Spatial analysis
- **Spatial autocorrelation** (queen contiguity on the 5 km training cells, 999 permutations): Moran's I of the event-cell indicator **0.19 (CA_N), 0.13 (CA_S), 0.28 (OR_WA)**, all p = 0.001 (the minimum of the permutation test); the features themselves are smooth (slope 0.75-0.86, rainfall 0.99, NDVI 0.76-0.87). Neighbouring cells are therefore not independent samples, which is why random CV is rejected and effective n is counted in blocks.
- **Event clustering** (primary training events): mean nearest-neighbour distance 6.9 km (CA_N), 4.0 km (CA_S), 3.7 km (OR_WA); Clark-Evans R **0.59 / 0.34 / 0.50** (1 = random, below 1 = clustered); 64% / 71% / 83% of events have another event within 5 km. Edge effects not corrected.
- **Reporting bias** (roads and city-distance data are not available): the share of cells with an event rises from **0.09% in the least built-up quintile to 12.6% in the most built-up quintile (about 140 times)**; the share does not change with distance to rivers (4.6-5.4% across quintiles). Region imbalance: OR_WA holds 51% of the training events but 29% of the training cells; CA_N 22% of the events and 32% of the cells.

## 6. Temporal analysis
- **Seasonality:** 65% of events fall in December-February, 88% in November-March (training part: January 104, February 86, March 87, December 109, November 20; only 3-13 per month from April to October). Event timing follows the Pacific wet season.
- **Rainfall relationship (monthly only):** for 451 training events from 2002 on, the rainfall of the month before the event month sits at a **median percentile of 0.625** of the same calendar month at the same IMERG cell (CA_N 0.63, CA_S 0.69, OR_WA 0.56); 33% are above the 75th and 16% above the 90th percentile (25% and 10% expected under no relation). A mild excess; the Wilcoxon p-value is below 1e-6 but events share storms, so it is descriptive only.
- **Lead-time relationships for the trigger task: not assessable.** Only monthly rainfall was acquired (no daily IMERG, no SMAP).
- **Event-date structure:** see section 3; the number of storm days is small relative to the number of events, so event-level independence is overstated.
- **Temporal hold-out feasibility** (training events; criterion: at least 150 events and 40 distinct dates on each side): cutoffs at the end of 2011, 2012, 2013 and 2014 meet it (for 2012: 231 train events / 133 dates vs 229 test events / 121 dates); 2009, 2010, 2015 and 2016 do not. **Allowed only with a label**: land cover, NDVI, rainfall climatology and soil are built from data after those cutoffs or are time-invariant, so any temporal hold-out is **not free of future information** for them (audit warning carried from M4).
- **Non-stationarity:** annual event counts rise by about 3 per year, but the GLC compiles news reports from 2007 on and counts are dominated by a few storm winters (2010-2011, 2017); the trend measures reporting effort and storms as much as landslide frequency.

## 7. Multicollinearity
68 testable features fall into 42 clusters at |Spearman| >= 0.9; **12 clusters hold more than one feature**: elevation mean/min/max; relief, elevation std, mean and 3rd-quartile slope and share above 15 degrees; maximum slope and share above 30 degrees; tree cover with NDVI mean and July-August NDVI; mean annual, winter and wettest-month rainfall; major-river mean/min distance; fault distances (all and young, mean and min); the four soil depths of coarse fragments, clay, sand and silt. VIF (4,603 complete cases, 49 features after dropping composition and exactly derived columns): **26 features above 10, 14 above 100**; the largest are winter rainfall 1,657, young-fault mean distance 1,274, annual rainfall 1,246, wettest-month rainfall 661, major-river distance 511, mean slope 240 (fig 5). The 0-30 cm soil means are exact linear mixes of the depth layers (VIF in the trillions if kept) and the land-cover, lithology and texture shares sum to 1, so one member of each composition must be dropped.

## 8. Physical sanity checks
Expected sign vs observed (pooled AUC, per-window sign): slope features as expected pooled but **reversed in OR_WA** (flagged); rainfall climatology as expected pooled but reversed in OR_WA (0.45); distance to major rivers as expected (0.40) but the interval includes 0.5 and CA_N disagrees; distance to all rivers 0.48 (no relation); clay 0.54, positive in CA_S/OR_WA, not CA_N; built-up share as expected for a reporting artefact. **Surprises flagged as artefact or confounding, not as leakage:** (i) every slope, relief and rainfall feature reverses in Oregon/Washington; (ii) the very strong built-up, soil-cover, water and fault-distance associations; (iii) none of 68 features has a single-feature AUC above 0.9 (the M4 leakage alarm stays quiet; the largest is 0.82).

## 9. Sensitivity of the ranking
- 10 km grid (training part: 189 event cells, 534 candidates): Spearman correlation of the feature AUCs with the 5 km ranking **0.977**, all top-10 features shared.
- Unknown-trigger events added (349 event cells): rank correlation **0.96**, 9 of 10 top features shared; built-up AUC 0.78.
- Flagged imprecise-circle candidates removed (1,920 candidates): rank correlation **0.88**, 7 of 10 shared; built-up AUC 0.81.
- Both together: 0.883, 4 of 10 shared; built-up AUC 0.77. The built-up dominance is robust to all three; the order below it is not.

## 11. Recommendation and decisions for you
**Recommended scope:** (1) **Task:** static susceptibility only, framed as a *ranking of relative propensity in an incomplete, reporting-biased inventory*; the trigger task is dropped from this study. (2) **Families:** prior, few-feature logistic baselines, regularised logistic regression, then shallow regularised GBM/RF; no deep model. (3) **Features:** two sets, decided in M6 and fixed before models are run: **A (primary, physical):** one representative per redundancy cluster from terrain (slope, elevation or relief, aspect), rainfall climatology, soil (clay, sand, coarse fragments 0-30 cm), lithology shares (one dropped), NDVI, distance to major rivers; **excluded from A as reporting proxies: built-up share, soil cover, water share, coastal lithology; distance to faults treated as confounded** (reported, tested in a separate set). **B (diagnostic):** A plus the proxies, only to measure how much of the apparent skill is reporting, never as the susceptibility claim. (4) **Evaluation:** spatial block CV with the sealed hold-out, reporting PR-AUC, recall at fixed false-alarm rates, Brier score, intervals from block bootstrap, per window and pooled, and leave-one-window-out. (5) **Compute budget (estimate, not measured; no model was fitted):** CPU only; xgboost and LightGBM CUDA builds work but data of about 7,000 training cells x 70 features gain nothing from a GPU. Logistic baselines minutes; nested spatial CV for GBM about 2-3 CPU-hours; the sensitivity variants (10 km grid, unknown-trigger set, unflagged background, feature set B) about 3 CPU-hours; bootstrap and SHAP about 1 CPU-hour: **about 6-10 CPU-hours in total (under one hour wall time on the 20 logical cores), 0 GPU-hours.**
**Decisions needed:** (a) approve susceptibility-only and dropping the trigger task; (b) approve the model families above and the exclusion of deep models; (c) approve feature sets A and B (including excluding built-up, soil cover, water share and coastal lithology from A, and treating fault distance as confounded); (d) approve the compute budget; (e) should I propose, for approval, a better reporting-bias covariate (population density such as GHSL or WorldPop, or an OSM roads subset; roads were declined earlier as too large) to use in model B or for bias correction? (f) accept the sealed hold-out (1d4a6741...) as the starting point for M6.

## 13. What was not verified, and risks
Not verified: the compute estimates (no model was run); the claim that fault-distance and built-up associations are reporting artefacts (consistent with the data, not proven; no population or road layer was available); lithology and soil vintages; whether the Oregon/Washington reversal also holds in the held-out part (deliberately unseen); Moran's I and Clark-Evans without edge corrections; the percentile analysis uses nearest 0.1 degree IMERG cells (about 10 km) for events located to 1-5 km. Risks: the inventory may be learned as a map of towns; effective n is about 53 blocks; temporal hold-out cannot be free of future information; the ranking beyond the top features is unstable under small, reasonable changes (section 9).
